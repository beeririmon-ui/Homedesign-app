One continuous, slow, perfectly straight sideways slider move inside an empty master bedroom. The camera looks square-on at one wall the whole time and slides 3.15 m to its left along that wall at one constant pace, sinking 15 cm on the way, 24 mm lens, level. It arrives exactly on the last frame.

The view direction never changes: the camera does not turn, it only slides. The open doorway in the wall, with the long corridor seen through it, travels across the frame to the right and leaves past the right edge. Inside the doorway the corridor shifts with real parallax: its near door frame moves with the wall, its far end lags behind and is covered by the door frame. The bed wall comes in from the left and the bed settles in the centre of the last frame.

Real parallax throughout: the foot of the bed, nearest the camera, moves fastest; the wall moves slower; what is seen through the door moves slowest. Exposure and white balance are locked.

Camera: a locked architectural camera on a straight slider. 24 mm throughout, no zoom, no focus change, everything sharp, horizon level, verticals vertical, no tilt, no roll, no pan, no shake, no arc, no push in. One constant speed.

Scene: the house is completely still and empty. Every wall, edge, plank, door and piece of furniture keeps its exact place, size, shape, colour and material from the two frames; nothing appears, disappears, melts, cross-fades or is redesigned. No people, no added objects, no lamps. Soft overcast winter daylight, constant. This is a physical camera move through real space, never a blend between two pictures.
